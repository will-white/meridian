# Phase 3 build plan: Investments

Source of truth: the design spec linked from `CLAUDE.md`. Four area designs were produced by independent designers, scored by judges and merged; a seam critic then read all four against the spec and the code. They live in `docs/design/phase-3/`:

- `imports-and-data.md`: tables, security master, SimpleFIN `extra` holdings, positions and trade CSV importers, manual entry, contributions, fidelity.
- `lots-and-performance.md`: the lot engine (FIFO, specific lot, splits, transfers), realized and unrealized gains, income by month, XIRR, true TWR and Modified Dietz, benchmark.
- `prices-and-valuation.md`: the price provider (Tiingo), the fetch job, implied prices, the valuation chain, value series, day and period change, reconciliation to a statement.
- `views-and-api.md`: Portfolio, Allocation (targets, drift, rebalance), Holdings, Performance, Income views; the income feed into the plan; dashboard additions; the web pages.
- `seams.md`: the critic's reconciled decisions (§1), every conflict with its resolution (§2), spec gaps (§3), Phase 1/2 contradictions (§4), the build order (§5).

Precedence: the spec, then **this document**, then `seams.md`, then the area design. Where an area design and `seams.md` disagree, `seams.md` wins; where this document and `seams.md` disagree, this document wins. An implementer builds from the area design for the algorithms, contracts and test cases, with the names and shapes below.

Scope (spec, Phase 3): securities, daily prices, holdings from SimpleFIN `extra` and positions CSV, trade-history import, lots and cost basis, allocation and performance views. Gate: account values match brokerage statements to the dollar on a chosen date. OFX/QFX import is **not** Phase 3 (the spec's data-sources table says phase 3, its build-phase list says Polish; the build list wins and the conflict is flagged); the importers consume `MappedPosition[]` / `MappedTrade[]` so an OFX adapter is a parser only.

## Reconciled decisions (binding)

Conventions carried from Phases 1 and 2: money is `bigint` cents; dates `YYYY-MM-DD`; months `YYYY-MM`; cents travel as decimal strings; `today` comes from the client within ±2 days of the household date (`resolveToday`); one audit batch per request and never when before equals after; typed service errors (`PlanError(status, code, message)`) mapped to HTTP by the routes; `not_found` with the entity named in the message for every 404.

1. **Numbers.** Share quantities and prices are `numeric(20,8)` in Postgres and **Fixed8** in TypeScript: `bigint` scaled by 10⁸, decimal strings on the wire (`Fixed8String = /^-?\d{1,12}(\.\d{1,8})?$/`, `NonNegFixed8String`; aliases `QuantityString`, `PriceString`). One module `packages/shared/src/decimal.ts` exports `parseFixed8`, `formatFixed8` (canonical, no trailing zeros), `roundDivHalfAway`, `mulToCents` (R1: quantity × price → cents), `quantityForCents` (R2: cents ÷ price → quantity), `impliedPrice` (R3: cents ÷ quantity → price, on absolute values), `allocateLargestRemainder` (R4), `scaleQty` (R5: split scaling), `fixed8ToCents`, `centsToFixed8`, `parseLooseDecimal`, `fixed8FromJsonNumber`, `percentToPpm`, `ppmToPercent`, `amountMismatch` (the 5-cent check shared by the trade preview and the engine), `RECONCILE_TOLERANCE_CENTS = 100n` and `matchesToDollar`. Rates (returns, ratios) are the only floats: computed, never stored, never fed back into cents.
2. **Rounding, stated once.** Half away from zero (`roundDivHalfAway`) at exactly these sites: R1, R2, R3, R5, `fixed8ToCents`, and the lot engine's basis consumption `roundDivHalfAway(basis × take, quantity)`. Cents split across pieces (proceeds across lots, class splits of a value) use largest remainder (R4). Every other integer division (basis points, ppm, target cents, pro-rata gain estimates, averages, monthly equivalents) keeps Phase 2's truncation toward zero. This is the one stated exception to the Phase 2 rule.
3. **Enums** (`packages/shared/src/enums.ts` is the single source; Drizzle text columns mirror them): `SecurityType = stock | etf | fund | bond | cash | cd | other`; `AssetClass = us_stock | intl_stock | bonds | cash | real_estate | other`; `PriceSource = provider | implied | manual` (also `security.price_mode`); `HoldingSource = simplefin | csv | manual`; `InvestmentTxnType = buy | sell | dividend | interest | reinvest | fee | split | transfer`; `InvestmentTxnSource = csv | manual | simplefin | ofx` (only the first two are written in Phase 3); `TransferDirection = in | out`; `LotMethod = fifo | specific`; `LotOpenedBy = buy | reinvest | transfer_in`; `GainTerm = short | long | unknown | none`; `DisposalKind = sell | transfer_out | unmatched_sell`; `InvestmentFidelity = balance_only | positions | full_history` (null for non-investment account types; there is no `none` member); `CashInference = auto | off`; `InvestmentIncomeFeed = off | link | create`; `CsvKind = bank | positions | trades`; `ValuationBasis = snapshot | lots | positions | balance | none`; `HoldingPriceSource = provider | implied | manual | statement | txn | cash`; `PriceStatus = statement | current | stale | implied | manual | cash | carried | missing` (the prices design's `fixed` is spelled `cash`); `LineKind = position | cash | cash_plug | carried_flows`; `InvestmentPeriod = 1w | 1m | 3m | 6m | ytd | 1y | 3y | 5y | all | custom` with one `periodStart`; `ReturnMethod = true_twr | modified_dietz`; `ExternalFlowKind = cash | securities | opening_value`; `ExternalFlowSource = ledger | investment_txn | engine`; `ReturnFailureReason` and `EngineWarningCode` per the lots design (`oversell`, `pick_unavailable`, `basis_unknown`, `stale_price`, …); `PriceProviderId = tiingo`; `PriceFetchStatus = ok | not_found | error | rate_limited`; `ReconcileRowStatus` per the prices design. Extended: `TransactionSource += investment`; `JobType += simplefin.reprocess_holdings, prices.fetch, investments.valuation, investments.income_feed`; `AuditEntity += security, investment_txn, holding_snapshot, allocation_target, lot_pick, account_reconciliation, account`; `ImportLogKind += csv.positions, csv.trades, simplefin.reprocess, prices.fetch`.

the normalized-name expression index; `security_cash_shape` CHECK), `security_alias` (imports), `security_allocation` (views: `(security_id, asset_class)` PK, `weight_bp` 1..10000), `price` (imports DDL with `close >= 0` and `source`, plus `adj_close`, `provider`, `suspect`, `fetched_at`, `updated_at` and the `price_provider_shape` CHECK; unique `(sell_txn_id, lot_id)`), `lot_disposal` (lots DDL incl. nullable `lot_id`, `kind`, `term`, `gain_cents`, `basis_known`; unique on `(close_txn_id, COALESCE(lot_id, '00000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000000'))`), `allocation_target` (`asset_class` PK, `target_bp`), `account_reconciliation` (prices DDL: several rows per account and date; Alterations: `account` gains `lot_method` (default `fifo`), `cash_inference` (default **`auto`**), `investment_income_feed` (default `off`) and **no** `investment_history`; `household_setting` gains `benchmark_security_id`, `investment_income_category_id`, `rebalance_band_bp` (default 500, 0..5000), `rebalance_min_trade_cents` (default 10000) and **no** `dividends_as_income`; `connection` gains `csv_kind` (default `bank`). No data backfill of any kind.
5. **Lots are a derived cache** rebuilt per account inside the writing transaction (`rebuildLots(tx, accountId)`, out-leg accounts of a transfer group first); lot ids are stable by `(opened_by_txn_id, ordinal)`; a transfer-in leg receives one lot per source lot (ordinals 0..n−1, `origin_lot_id`), or one lot with `basis_known = false` when no counterparty exists. Unknown basis is `basis_known = false` with basis 0 in the table and the engine, `null` on the wire. Specific identification uses `lot_pick` rows (Σ picks ≤ sale, FIFO for the remainder, `needs_lot_pick` warning when the method is `specific` and no picks exist); `CreateInvestmentTxnBody.lotPicks` writes them in the same request. Realized gains are read from `lot_disposal`; proceeds split across lots by largest remainder; split scaling R5 per lot without a residual fix (the 0.001-share position tolerance absorbs it, in the position check and in disposals: audit decision A3 below). Same-day order is `(date, type rank, sequence, id)` with rank `split < transfer in < buy < reinvest < dividend/interest < fee < sell < transfer out`; `rowOrder` only orders same-rank rows.
6. **Reinvested dividends are two rows**: a `dividend` (income, cash +) and a `reinvest` (`amount_cents ≤ 0`, a purchase, cash −). The engine treats `reinvest` as a buy with basis `−amount` and never as income; `reinvestedCents` per month = Σ `−amount` of reinvest rows. Trade mappings carry `reinvestRows` (`separate` default, `combined` per institution) so either file convention lands as two rows; a reinvest-looking row without a quantity is income only.
7. **Fidelity is derived, never stored**: `accountFidelities(db, accountIds)` in `packages/db/src/services/accountFidelity.ts` = `hasTrades` / `hasSnapshots` plus the engine's `checkPositions` (0.001-share tolerance; a mismatch between the lots and the latest snapshot yields `positions` with a note). The check skips the cash security, securities with `asset_class = 'cash'` and dollar-only rows. `AccountView.investment = { fidelity, fidelityNote, positionsMatch, lastSnapshotDate, lastSnapshotSource, lastTradeDate, securityCount, needsReviewCount, lastReconciliation }` (null for non-investment types), plus `lotMethod`, `cashInference`, `investmentIncomeFeed` on the account.
8. **One valuation chain** `valueAccountOn` (prices design, `packages/shared/src/valuation.ts`) defines an investment account's value on a date for every consumer (Portfolio, Holdings, Allocation, the dashboard investments block, the reconcile preview, day and period change, and the performance engine's valuation points for non-full accounts): lots (only when derived fidelity is `full_history` and the latest trade checkpoint is newer than the latest snapshot; a position without a close is priced at the account's latest trade price: audit decision A20 below) → the latest holding snapshot on or before the date, repriced after its date → the reported balance → `none`. Cash is anchored the same way for every basis: `anchor` = latest reported `balance_snapshot` (source ≠ `computed`) on or before the date; `cash_plug = anchor.balance − Σ positions priced at anchor.date`, carried forward as a `cash_plug` line labelled "Cash (from balance)" whatever its sign; a negative plug is also reported as a `negative_residual` warning (audit decision A1 below replaces the earlier "never negative cash"); transfers after the anchor are carried as cash (`carried_flows`). `cash_inference = off` disables the plug and the carried flows for that account; the reconcile preview computes both settings. Holding value: the snapshot's own value on its date, `mulToCents(quantity, close)` when a price strictly newer than the snapshot exists; dollar-only rows always at the snapshot value. Cash securities are valued at 1 and never priced.
9. **Computed balance rows.** A worker job `investments.valuation` writes `balance_snapshot` rows with `source = 'computed'` from the chain (dates from the last reported row through the expected price date; `ON CONFLICT … WHERE source = 'computed'`, never overwriting a reported row) after every `prices.fetch` run, positions commit, manual snapshot and reconcile commit. Phase 2's net worth stays on `balance_snapshot` unchanged and equals the Portfolio on every date; the engine excludes computed rows from cash anchors.
10. **Prices.** Provider interface in `packages/shared/src/prices.ts` (pure parsing, symbol mapping, suspect rule, `expectedPriceDate` on the America/New_York clock via `localClockInTimeZone` and the NYSE calendar in `marketCalendar.ts`) with one adapter `apps/worker/src/prices/tiingo.ts`. `PRICE_PROVIDER = tiingo | none` (default `none`, which keeps the app correct on implied and manual prices and never queues a fetch); `TIINGO_API_KEY` from env or a secret file, never the database. The job `prices.fetch { reason: holdings | nightly | manual }` runs once a night after the US close in the household timezone plus one conditional catch-up, for held securities, the benchmark, `always_price` securities and any security with a `lot_disposal` dated within 400 days; held = `listHeldSecurityIds(db)`; only `price_mode = 'provider'` is fetched using `provider_symbol ?? ticker`; budget 45 requests per run spaced 1.5 s, 20-minute wall-clock cap, continuations deduped per root job, 3-day overlap, per-security exponential backoff, 3 attempts per symbol, `Retry-After` capped at 120 s; failures are recorded on `security.price_fetch_*` and shown by `GET /api/prices/status` and the Prices page (no alert rows). Every price refresh goes through `requestPriceRefresh` (which uses the new `enqueueJob` `payload.dedupeKey`). Price precedence `manual > provider > implied` through one `upsertPrices`; implied prices are written for every valued position lacking a provider or manual price by `rebuildImpliedPrices(tx, { securityIds, dates })` (deterministic tie rule: largest `value_cents`, then `account_id`; the price of a row is `statementPrice`, never a 0 beside a value: audit decision A4 below). Adjusted closes are a column; the benchmark uses them when present (`adjusted: boolean` reported, `priceOnly` in the UI otherwise). User-facing staleness is counted in trading days against the expected close and applies only to provider-mode securities, and only while a provider is configured (audit decision A18 below); the engine's 7-day `stale_price` stays an engine warning.
11. **External flows** come from one loader `loadExternalFlows(db, accountIds, from, to)`: posted ledger `is_transfer` rows ∪ `investment_txn` cash transfers (a cash row is dropped when the ledger row it is linked to is itself such a flow, or, unlinked, when an equal-amount ledger transfer within 3 days exists; a cash row linked to a bank row that is not a posted transfer is the flow itself: audit decision A5 below) ∪ securities transfers at market ∪ opening values of accounts entering after `from`; each flow carries `accountId`, `kind` and `source`. Contributions are timed at the start of their day, withdrawals at the end of theirs (audit decision A2 below replaces "start-of-day timing" for money and shares that leave); true TWR (chain-linked sub-periods) for full-history accounts, Modified Dietz otherwise, the method named in the output; XIRR per Microsoft's definition with a deterministic bracket and bisection (tolerance 1e-10), typed failure reasons; annualized only when the range is ≥ 365 days; the benchmark is a shadow portfolio buying `cents ÷ close` units with every flow.
12. **Day change** = price movement on today's quantities (balance-only accounts excluded with a reason; the total marked partial). **Period change** = one computation: `changeCents`, `netFlowsCents` (external flows + opening values), `growthCents = change − netFlows`, plus `incomeCents` and `uncategorizedRows`; `flows_not_in_value` reason when a positions-basis account with cash inference off has flows in the window. The views design's "why did it change" attribution (price effect + quantity effect + residual + flows, summing exactly to Δ) stays for arbitrary windows.
13. **Income.** One route `GET /api/investments/income` (views design) built on the engine's pure `incomeByMonth(txns)`; months carry dividends, interest, fees and `reinvestedCents`. The feed into the plan is per account (`investment_income_feed`): `link` categorizes the bank twin of a dividend/interest row into `household_setting.investment_income_category_id` (an income-bucket category; unlocked rows only, like the rules engine); `create` adds synthetic ledger rows `source = 'investment'` keyed `inv:<investment_txn id>` for accounts without a bank feed and an upgrade pass replaces them when the bank row arrives; reinvested dividends are never fed (a fed row leaves when its reinvest row arrives, audit decision A12); feed rows are excluded from transfer pairing and recurring detection; the Phase 2 plan loaders read feed-category rows from off-budget accounts. The job `investments.income_feed` runs after syncs, trade imports, feed-setting changes and the daily tick.
14. **Reconciliation** (the gate): the prices design's `account_reconciliation` table, `POST /api/reconcile/preview` (readOnly, writes nothing), `POST /api/reconcile/commit` (owner; always records the attempt; with `accept = true` writes the statement as a `manual` holding snapshot, replaces that date's balance row, rebuilds implied prices, enqueues the valuation job, audits one batch), `GET /api/reconcile/history`, `DELETE /api/reconcile/:id`; audit entity `account_reconciliation`. `matchesToDollar = |diff| < 100` cents everywhere (imports preview, reconcile, dashboard); a difference of 0 is shown as "exact". Positions imports write `balance_snapshot = statementTotalCents ?? Σ positions` for accounts without a SimpleFIN feed (`PositionsCommitBody` and `ManualPositionsBody` gain optional `statementTotalCents`) and only on request for SimpleFIN-fed ones. The sync's balance upsert gains `WHERE source <> 'manual'` so a hand-checked statement survives the next sync; snapshot precedence `manual > csv > simplefin` applies to holding and balance rows alike.
15. **Contributions.** The ingest-time classifier marks inflows into investment-type accounts `is_transfer = true` (unpaired transfers, as the rules engine may); trade-file cash rows become `investment_txn` cash transfers and, for accounts without a SimpleFIN connection, also ledger rows (`is_transfer`, Phase 1 ids) linked by `ledger_transaction_id`; a manual cash transfer into such an account creates the ledger row the same way, so hand-entered 401(k) deferrals reach savings goals. A bank row that a cash transfer is linked to is marked `is_transfer` at that moment, whatever its description, unless a rule said it is not a transfer or the household categorized it by hand (audit decision A5 below), so a deposit the classifier did not recognise, and every withdrawal, still counts. Investment-type accounts default `on_budget = false` at creation (no migration backfill; the Accounts page offers a one-click move); Phase 2's `loadActuals`, `loadUncategorized` and `countUncategorized` gain `account.on_budget = true OR category = feed category`; `loadGoalNets` is unchanged.
16. **Allocation.** `security.asset_class` is the primary class; `security_allocation` splits (Σ 10000 bp, primary must be a split class, cash securities never split) refine blended funds; household targets in `allocation_target` replaced as a whole set (`400 allocation_sum`); allocation covers positions and full-history accounts only (balance-only accounts shown as unclassified); drift against `rebalance_band_bp`; the deterministic greedy rebalance (tax rank `pre_tax = hsa = roth < none < taxable`, then value descending; per-account sells fund buys; never moves money across accounts; minimum trade `rebalance_min_trade_cents`; pro-rata gain estimate on taxable sells). Holdings rows per security across accounts carry cost basis with coverage `full | partial | none` and `basisSource ∈ lots | statement`, the integer-ppm weighted expense ratio with `coveredBp`, and concentration warnings (stock ≥ 10% warn, ≥ 25% bad; a single fund ≥ 60% info). Shares without a known basis are uncovered (audit decision A11); inferred cash always has a row, and the expense ratio and concentration weigh the rows of positive value (A13, A17).
17. **Routes and files** (one owner each): imports → `routes/securities.ts`, `routes/investmentTxns.ts`, additions to `routes/import.ts` (positions and trades preview/commit, `connection.csv_kind`); lots → `routes/performance.ts` (`POST /api/investments/performance` readOnly with `detail`, `GET /api/investments/lots` (the lots as they stood on `asOf`: audit decision A6 below), `GET /api/investments/realized`, `PUT /api/investments/txns/:id/lot-picks`, `POST /api/investments/lots/rebuild`); prices → `routes/prices.ts` (`GET /api/prices/status`, `POST /api/prices/refresh`, `PUT/DELETE /api/prices/:securityId/:date`), `routes/valuation.ts` (`GET /api/valuation/summary`, `GET /api/valuation/series`), `routes/reconcile.ts`; views → `routes/investmentViews.ts` (`GET /api/investments/portfolio | allocation | holdings | performance | income | changes`, `PUT /api/investments/securities/:id/allocation`, `GET/PUT /api/investments/allocation-targets` is the imports area's). One `PATCH /api/investments/securities/:id` whose pricing fields call `updateSecurityPricing`; manual prices only at `PUT/DELETE /api/prices/:securityId/:date`. `asOf` is the date parameter on every GET; `accountIds` is comma-separated on GETs; the engine's POST keeps its body array and a test asserts the GET presentation equals the POST. View mappers live beside the routes under `apps/api/src/views/`.
18. **Jobs.** `enqueueJob` gains `payload.dedupeKey` (existing callers unchanged); the runner retries unless `err.permanent === true`; `investments.valuation` is chained from the end of every `prices.fetch` run and enqueued (deduped) after positions commits, manual snapshots and reconcile commits; `DAILY_JOB_TYPES = recurring.detect, investments.income_feed, plan.close_month`; `schedulePriceJobs` runs separately on the household-local clock; `simplefin.reprocess_holdings` replays stored `import_log` payloads through the holdings parser. The sync applies holdings per account (`account.holdings` and five `extra` shapes, raw payload kept, `holdings.unrecognized` / `partial` reported per account, never dated by the clock), then `linkUnlinkedInvestmentTxns`, then enqueues the income feed.
19. **Viewer role.** Every read is a GET (or a `readOnly` POST, the cash-flow precedent); mutations are refused by the auth plugin; `AuthStatus` gains `role`; the web hides editors. (Audit: the one exception is a member's own sign-in, routes marked `ownAccount`; how viewers are created and managed is in `docs/phase-1-plan.md`, audit decisions 18 to 22.)

## Build order

Steps 1–2 are the skeleton (task 14); 3–12 the implementation workflow (task 15); 13 the gate (task 16). Each step is buildable and testable alone; `seams.md` §5 gives the file lists.

5. Import parsers (`simplefinHoldings.ts`, `investmentsCsv.ts`), in parallel with 4.
6. Security master, snapshots and prices services.
7. Investment transactions, lots service, fidelity, and the Phase 1/2 ingest touches.
8. Valuation, performance and series services.
9. Reconcile, views, allocation and income services; the INV seed; settings, dashboard, pairing and recurring touches.
10. Worker.
11. API.
12. Web.
13. Gate: `investmentsGate.test.ts` (positions CSV → implied prices → fake-provider fetch → reconcile preview within $1 → commit → post-commit exact → dashboard `awaitingReconcile` 0, on both seeds) plus the API-level end-to-end test; both also value full-history accounts from their lots after the last statement (trades, a dividend, a withdrawal, a margin debit) against a hand-made next statement, and no statement is accepted before the app's own value matched it to the dollar (audit decision A8 below); full test suite, typecheck, lint, build, smoke; commit and push. The owner reconciling one real account to a real statement is the acceptance step.

## Deviations from the spec (flagged)

- OFX/QFX import deferred to Polish (build list over data-sources table).
- Rounding half away from zero at the six named sites (decision 2), an exception to Phase 2's truncation rule.
- `investment_txn.type` gains `interest`; `security.type` gains `other`; columns beyond the spec rows on every table (idempotent imports, ordering, corporate actions, transfers, editable imported rows, fetch state, adjusted closes).

## Recorded during implementation

- Valuation: on the positions basis the cash plug is not anchored on a reported balance older than the snapshot in use (a `negative_residual` warning names the date instead); the period change reports `partial` and `missingAccountIds` when an account valued at the start cannot be valued at the end; the valuation job rewrites computed balance rows from the earliest computed row forward, and dates between two older reported rows that never received computed rows stay unfilled (an owner call on widening the window to the first data date).
- Reconciliation: a total-only accept (no rows) records a manual balance for the date and never deletes the positions; the preview computes the alternative cash-inference reading in the off → auto direction.
- Transactions: a ledger row is treated as derived from an investment cash transfer only when the service wrote it (`inv:<id>` external id or the same import log), so a bank row merely linked to a transfer never has its date or amount rewritten and is never deleted (it is marked a transfer when the link is made, unless a rule or a hand category says otherwise: audit decision A5 below); paired cash transfers mirror amount and date edits onto the counterpart leg, and paired share transfers their date (audit decision A7 below).
- Imports: the trade-action classifier reads the trade verbs (bought, sold, reinvest) before the income words, so a fund whose name contains "dividend" or "interest" still imports as a trade; an income word decides only when no trade verb is present or the row has no quantity (imports design §3.8 lists the older order).

## Decisions made during the audit

These were taken while fixing the Phase 1–3 audit findings. They are binding like the reconciled decisions above and, where they differ from `seams.md` or an area design in `docs/design/phase-3/`, they win.

Investment engines (findings p3-02, p3-05, p3-04, p3-09):

- **A1. Inferred cash may be negative; the value on a reported balance's date is that balance.** Decision 8 and the prices, views and seams designs said a balance below the positions priced on its date is "a `negative_residual` warning, never negative cash". That dropped a margin debit as soon as a trade was newer than the last statement (a 10,000.00 account shown as 15,500.00), valued an account above its own statement total when the positions file had no cash line, made the Portfolio disagree with the Performance page (whose `valueFullAccount` always kept signed cash, the lots design's D19) and with Phase 2's net worth (which reads the reported row), and was one-sided: a balance above the positions raised the value, a balance below them did not lower it. Now the plug is carried as the `cash_plug` line "Cash (from balance)" whatever its sign, in class cash, and a negative one is also a `negative_residual` warning that says what was done and what to do ("…counted as negative cash (a margin loan, or holdings that changed or lost value since the last statement; turn the account's cash inference off if the balance is not its total value)"). The stale-anchor rule is unchanged: a balance older than the statement in use plugs nothing of either sign and only warns. Consequences: `valueAccountOn` and `valueFullAccount` agree on every date with a balance; the Allocation's cash class and the Portfolio's "unlisted" figure can be negative (shown as "− $5,000.00 unlisted"); the valuation job's computed rows after a low reported balance follow that balance. Chosen over making the performance engine drop negative cash too, because the spec's gate is the statement total and a margin account's statement total is its positions less the debit.
- **A2. Withdrawals leave at the end of their day; contributions still arrive at the start of theirs.** Decision 11 and the lots design's D16 timed every flow at the start of its day, so a withdrawal was subtracted from the previous close. An account emptied in one day then kept a sliver of capital (previous close less the amount leaving) that lost 100 %, or a negative one: moving a whole position to another broker in kind gave −100 % or `negative_capital` (a partial move, +350 % in the auditor's scenario), and so did selling everything and withdrawing the proceeds the same day, and so did a balance-only account rolled over the day after a balance (Modified Dietz; an exact withdrawal was `zero_denominator`). Now, in true TWR, a boundary falls the day before every contribution and on the day of every withdrawal, `begin = V(b₀) + contributions dated b₀ + 1`, `end = V(b₁) + |withdrawals dated b₁|`; in Modified Dietz a withdrawal's weight is `dayDiff(date, b₁) / D` (a contribution's stays `(dayDiff + 1) / D`). This matches the lot engine's own same-day order (transfers in first, sells before transfers out). Securities transfers are still valued "at market on their date" (the row's price, else the latest close on or before it) on both legs, so an in-kind move happens at that day's close: the sending account's return includes the day, the receiving account's starts after it, the two chain to the household's, and the paired legs still cancel in the total and in the period change. `SubPeriod` and `SubPeriodView` gain `outflowCents` (the withdrawals among `flowCents`, ≤ 0) and the Performance detail shows "In" and "Out" columns. Chosen over repricing in-kind transfers at the previous close (the auditor's first suggestion) because that fixes only shares, not cash, and would make the two legs' amounts depend on which account has a price history.
- **A3. The 0.001-share tolerance applies to disposals, and a lot never rounds to nothing.** Decision 5 left split rounding to "the 0.001-share position tolerance", which only the fidelity check used. Now a sale or transfer out within 0.001 shares of the whole open position disposes of exactly the position: a hair above it is not an `oversell` with an `unmatched_sell` piece (the proceeds go to the matched lots), a hair below it leaves no dust lot (the dust and its basis go with the disposal); a pick within the tolerance of its lot, above or below it, takes exactly the lot without `pick_exceeds_lot` and accounts for the shares it names (a hair it names beyond the lot is not taken from another lot, a hair of the lot below it is not left open; the FIFO remainder is the sale less the picks), and the lot-pick validation of `POST /api/investments/transactions` and `PUT …/lot-picks` accepts a pick up to 0.001 shares above the lot's open quantity (a lot closed as of the sale still takes no pick), so the statement's 0.3336 shares can be picked from a lot a split left at 0.33333333 (second round of the audit; the first round's engine capped such a pick at the lot and sent the rest FIFO, and the route refused it); a transfer-in whose source pieces differ from the row's quantity by the tolerance opens the source lots and no unknown-basis remainder. A sale with no open lot at all is still unmatched, however small. A lot a reverse split would round to 0 shares keeps one unit (0.00000001) so its basis stays on an open lot until a disposal realizes it (before, it was closed with its basis on it and no disposal). There is still no residual fix at the split itself.
- **A4. A positions row is stored with the price its value implies unless the reported price produces that value.** One helper `statementPrice(quantity, price, valueCents)` (shared `decimal.ts`) is used by the positions CSV mapper, the SimpleFIN holdings parser and `replaceHoldingSnapshot` (so the positions editor and the reconcile commit obey it too): the reported price stands when `quantity × price` is within max(1 cent, 0.5 %) of the reported value (`valueMismatch`), otherwise the row carries `impliedPrice(value, quantity)` and keeps its `value_mismatch` warning; an absent price is implied as before; a value of exactly 0 implies nothing (the reported price stands, a worthless position keeps 0); cash rows are a dollar a unit and are left alone. This replaces the imports design's "price as reported, mismatch only flagged": an export with 0.00 in the price column (or a stale or mis-mapped price column) wrote that number as the security's implied close, which valued the same fund at $0 in every other account. `rebuildImpliedPrices` derives each close as the row's `statementPrice` too and never a close of 0 from a row with a non-zero value, so a row stored before this rule with a price that does not produce its value (a 0.00, a stale or a mis-mapped column) heals to value ÷ quantity on the next rebuild (second round of the audit: the first round healed only a price of exactly 0). The stored row keeps its price until its statement is imported again; on its own date the row's value is what counts.

Investment flows and performance (findings p3-01, p3-06, p3-10, p3-11):

- **A5. A bank row that a cash transfer is linked to is a transfer; a linked deposit never vanishes.** The linker (`linkToLedger`) matched an investment cash transfer to any posted bank row of the same amount within 3 days and left the row as it was, while the flow loader dropped every linked cash row on the assumption that its bank row was the flow. Only rows marked `is_transfer` are flows, and the ingest classifier marks inflows by a fixed list of words and never an outflow. So a deposit the bank calls "MONEYLINK TXN FROM CHK 1234", and every withdrawal whose other side is not a connected account, disappeared once the trade history named it: the account was valued without the money (or, with a later statement, the money was reported as growth, +500 % in the auditor's case), the linked goal was not credited, and nothing warned. Now (1) linking a cash transfer marks the bank row a transfer exactly as pairing marks its legs (`markAsTransfers`: `is_transfer`, and the category goes unless the owner locked it), on every path: a trade import, a manual entry, the pass after a sync, a link set by hand (`PATCH … ledgerTransactionId`) and a row that becomes a cash transfer while linked; the bank row's date, amount and description are still never touched, and rows linked to dividends, interest or fees are left alone. An automatic mark takes only the rows automatic pairing takes (audit second round, `docs/audit/phases-1-3/b3-investment-flows.md`): a row a rule said is not a transfer (`not_transfer`, Phase 1 decision 106) and a row the household categorized by hand that is not a transfer (`category_locked`, Phase 1 decision 48: automation never overrides a hand categorization) stay as they are, linked, and their investment rows are the flows by (2). Before, the linker marked a ruled-out row and the next rules run unmarked it, so the goal's month changed with whether rules had run since the last import or sync, and it took a hand-categorized row out of the plan. The ledger rows a trade import writes for the cash rows of an account without a feed keep a rule's "not a transfer" the same way (the rules run on them first). A link the household sets by hand is its word about that bank row, as pairing by hand is (decisions 52 and 106): it marks the row whatever a rule said (`not_transfer` cleared) and keeps a hand category on it. A row that becomes a cash transfer under a link the linker made is an automatic mark. (2) The flow loader drops a linked cash row only when its bank row is itself a flow (a posted transfer of the same account); otherwise the investment row is the flow, and no other ledger row is taken for its twin. So when the owner later says "not a transfer" on the bank row, the ledger and the goal follow the owner (the row leaves the goal) while the account's value and return still count the deposit; an edit that leaves the link and the kind alone never re-marks the row. (3) Migration `0005_linked_cash_transfers` repairs links made before this once, the same way; it also marked hand-categorized rows, as this decision first said. No later migration undoes that, because such a mark cannot be told from the household's own: the row kept its category, and "not a transfer" on it puts it back in the plan. A row marked over a rule's "not a transfer" before the second round returns to the rule's choice at the next rules run, and stays there. Chosen over only teaching the loader (the auditor's second option) because goals read the ledger (decision 15, spec: contributions "are recognized as transfers and counted toward savings goals"), and over widening the classifier's word list because no list covers every bank's wording, and outflows cannot be classified by words at all. Decision 11's "dropped when `ledger_transaction_id` is set" and the imports design's decision 14(c) are replaced by this.
- **A6. Holdings, lots and gains of a performance result, and the lots list, are the state on `asOf`.** The engine built a full-history account's lots from all its rows and reported that final state priced on `asOf`, so a window ending before a later sale showed the right end value beside today's smaller holdings and a "realized" gain from the future (the Performance page sends `asOf = to` for custom periods); `GET /api/investments/lots?asOf=` likewise listed today's lots priced on the date. Now `computePerformance` takes the lot engine's state at the end of `asOf` (a build over the rows dated on or before it, only when a later row exists; same picks, transfer sources and lot ids) for `holdings`, `lots` and the all-history `gains` summary; `realized` stays the window's `(from, to]`; `disposals` lists every disposal dated on or before the later of `to` and `asOf`, so the list covers both. `listLots` runs the engine through `asOf` per account instead of reading the stored cache (the cache is the same state when `asOf` is today), so quantities, remaining basis, closed dates and the valued columns of a `detail: full` response all describe that date, and each lot keeps its stored id. The loader still hands the engine every row (the position check compares the lots with the latest statement, which may be newer than the window). The lot pick dialog, which lists the lots as of the sale date, now labels the quantity "Left that day" (after the row under its current picks or FIFO); the server's dry rebuild remains the authority for what a pick may take. Chosen over renaming the parameter to a price date (the auditor's alternative) because the lots design's §3.14 and §3.15 define holdings and the gain summary "as of `asOf`".
- **A7. A paired share transfer has one date once the owner edits it.** `PATCH` already mirrored an amount or date change across a paired cash transfer; on a paired share transfer the date could change on one leg alone, so the receiving account held the shares before they left the source (double counted in the household total) or after (missing from it). Now a date change on either leg moves the other leg to the same date in the same request (audited in the batch; an imported leg becomes `user_edited`, so a re-import does not move it back), and both accounts' lots are rebuilt. What moved (type, security, quantity, direction) still cannot change while paired. Two imported legs that the files date up to 3 days apart keep their own dates until the owner edits one: the pairing window is unchanged, and an import never rewrites the other account's leg. Chosen over refusing the edit (`transfer_leg_linked`) because cash legs already mirror and a refusal would force unpairing, which drops the carried lots.
- **A8. The gate never accepts a statement the app has not matched.** The API gate recorded a dividend on a positions-level account and then accepted a statement the app still missed by 12.40, and neither gate compared a value built from lots, after the last statement, with a statement. Now the API gate previews three times on Oct 20 (12.40 short on the cash row; still 12.40 short after the dividend is recorded, because a positions-level account takes its cash from statements; one cent short once the trade history is imported and the lots explain the cash) and accepts only then; a further step values the account from its lots after a sale, a buy, a dividend and a withdrawal and previews the next statement to the cent (0.00) before the commit, with the realized gain, the remaining basis and the two-period time-weighted return checked by hand; the service gate has the same lots-basis case on its own account. The margin (negative cash) cases were added with A1. That a recorded dividend does not raise a positions-level account's value until a statement or the trade history covers it is the documented chain (decision 8: on the positions basis only transfers are carried) and is now asserted rather than passed over; carrying recorded income on the positions basis as well is left as an owner call (open question 7).
- **Holdings page, found while testing A6.** The inferred cash lines of one account (the plug, the trades' cash, the carried transfers) shared one cost-basis key, so the Cash row of the Holdings page showed the last line's value as every line's basis (a 0.00 cash position with a 2,000.00 "basis" and a −2,000.00 "gain" in the test). Each inferred line now has its own key; cash has basis = value and no gain.

Import pages (findings web-02, web-03):

- **A9. A positions preview asks for the statement date; it never refuses a file for lacking one.** The imports design answered `POST /api/import/positions/preview` with 400 `as_of_required` when the mapping takes the date from the request and none was sent, and put the date field in the mapping editor, which the page can only draw from a preview response: the reference file of the design (a Fidelity-style export, no date column) showed an error and nothing to type the date into, and a saved profile for such a file did the same. Now the preview reads the file without a date (`readPositionsCsvRows`) and answers 200 with the headers, the sample rows, the mapping (guessed, or the profile's), the account-filter values, the unreadable rows, `asOf: null` and the new `asOfRequired: true`; `positions` is empty and the totals are zero until there is a date, because nothing is compared with a stored snapshot or stored without one. The same answer is given when a date column yields no readable date in any row (a blank or mis-formatted column): the rows say why and the page says to pick another column or format, or to enter the date. A snapshot is still never dated by the clock, and the commit still answers 400 `as_of_required`. Chosen over a date field that is always shown beside the file picker (most files that have a date column would show a field that does nothing, and the first preview would still be a red error), and over handling the 400 on the page (the error carries neither the headers nor the mapping, so the editor could not be drawn and a wrongly guessed date column could not be corrected).
- **A10. One preview per change, on all three import flows, and what is on screen stays there when a change is refused.** The bank flow (Phase 1) re-ran its preview from an effect that depended on the mapping object and stored each response's mapping, a new object every time: about a hundred requests a second, the mapping and preview cards never stayed mounted, and no import could be committed from the page. All three flows now share `useCsvPreview` (`apps/web/src/components/import/`): the preview is a query keyed by the request (`previewQueryOptions`; the request builders are pure, in `lib/importMapping.ts`), so it runs once per change of the file, account, profile, mapping, date, filter or choices; the page takes the server's mapping only while it has none of its own, and files the response under the request that names that mapping, so the guess costs no second request (the positions and trades flows used to ask twice). A new file gets a key that is unique while the app is open (`nextFileKey`; a per-page counter restarted when the page was reopened and two files of one length could share a cached preview) and never shows another file's rows. When the server refuses a change (unchecking "first row is a header" with columns named by header, a choice that no longer exists), the last preview of the file stays on screen under the error with the commit disabled, so the owner can undo it; before, the editor that held the control went away with the response. A commit invalidates the previews, which then show the rows as already imported (bank, trades) or the snapshot as stored (positions); the bank result line keeps the duplicate count taken at the commit. (Second round of the audit.) What the hook keeps is the pure `nextKept` and `keptFor` of `previewQuery.ts` (the last settled answer of the chosen file, the same object while nothing changes, never another file's), and `useCsvPreview.test.ts` runs the hook itself under a minimal hooks runtime that keeps React's rules for state and effect dependencies (the web package has no DOM test environment): one request per change, the guess taken without a second request, the last answer kept after a refusal and never shown for another file.

Investment views (findings b6-investment-views-01 to -16):

- **A11. Shares without a known basis are not covered, and never gain.** The Holdings view took the lot engine's `costBasisCents` (the known lots only, lots design §3.14) and set the gain to the whole line's value less it, and called any sub-row with a basis `full` coverage. Ten shares transferred in without a basis then showed as $3,125.00 of gain (+44.7 % instead of +15.7 %), with no partial marker on the row, and the rebalance's taxable-sell estimate started from the same figure. Lots design D12 says such a gain is unknown and that presenting it as a gain would mislead. Now a sub-row's covered value is the known-basis lots' own value (`knownLotsCoverage`): the basis plus the engine's `unrealizedCents` when the engine priced the lots at the line's close, else the known shares (`quantity − unknownBasisQuantity`) at the line's price; its gain is that value less the basis, the engine's figure; a sub-row whose lots hold shares without a basis, or whose known lots hold a different number of shares than the statement line (beyond the position check's 0.001 shares), makes the row `partial`; the row's `gainCents` and `gainBp` sum and divide covered figures only (views design §3.8 unchanged otherwise). A line without a share count or price cannot be compared with the lots: it is covered whole when every lot has a basis, else uncovered. (Second round of the audit: the first round took the unknown shares off the statement's share count rather than the lots', so when the lots disagreed with the statement, the `lots_mismatch` case whose basis the views design keeps showing, the known lots' +1,700.00 read −1,425.00, a statement holding fewer shares than the unknown ones read −100 %, and the rebalance estimated a loss on a sell of lots that were all in gain; and lots covering 20 of a statement's 40 shares counted the other 20 as gain, +7,500.00 where the lots hold +1,250.00.) The rebalance takes the known lots' gain, and a sell of a line with unknown-basis shares says "(not counting N sh whose basis is unknown)". Chosen over excluding a partly known line altogether (the known lots' gain is real and the engine reports it) and over valuing the unknown shares at zero basis (which is the defect).
- **A12. A reinvested dividend leaves the plan when its reinvest row arrives.** Decision 13 and the views design's D15 say reinvested dividends are never fed, but the feed only skipped them when deciding what to feed: a dividend fed in `create` mode before its reinvest row was recorded kept its synthetic row in the income category, so the plan counted income that never arrived as cash. Now `unfeedReinvestedDividends` deletes the account's unlocked synthetic rows of dividends that have a reinvest row of the same account, security and date; every feed pass runs it first (counted in `deleted`), and the investment-transaction writers run it the moment a reinvest row is written (manual entry, an edit, a trade import), so nothing waits for the worker. A deleted or re-dated reinvest row queues a feed pass, which feeds the dividend again. A bank row linked to a reinvested dividend that carries the feed category unlocked (the feed put it there: a category set by hand is locked) is given back what the household's own rules give it, the rule's category, else the payee's default, else none, which is what it carries when the reinvest row comes first; when the rules themselves give it the feed category it keeps it (second round of the audit: the first round left the feed's category on such a bank row, in `link` mode and on a synthetic row already upgraded to its bank twin, so a reinvested 49.10 was September plan income or not depending on whether the feed ran before the reinvest row arrived). Both count in the feed's `deleted`, the fed rows taken out of the plan. A bank row whose category the owner set by hand, and a synthetic row whose category the owner locked, stay, as when the feed is turned off. An unlinked reinvested dividend reads `not_fed`, not `awaiting`.
- **A13. Inferred cash always has a Holdings row, and the cash row says what it holds.** The cash lines without a security (the balance plug, the lots basis' trade cash, carried transfers) joined the cash security's row, and were dropped when the household had none (a SimpleFIN brokerage that lists positions but never a cash line, so the importers never create one): the rows no longer summed to the total, the weights and the expense ratio were off, and a developer's sentence was the only trace. Now they form a Cash row of their own, with `securityId` null (the contract allows it for this row only), so the rows always sum to the total (D4). The views stay read-only: no security is created by a read. The cash row's quantity is its whole value at 1.00 a unit (it showed the reported line's 270 units for $1,702.00), each sub-row carries its line's name and whether it is inferred, and the page labels trade cash "from trades (since the last balance)" and carried transfers "transfers since the last balance" instead of "reported" or "unlisted"; the Cash stat shows the cash row's total with what is reported and what is inferred. The class editor is not offered on the cash row (always class cash, never split).
- **A14. Income periods follow the income rows.** The Income view's `all` started at the first valuation date, so dividends from an activity export that predates the first statement were dropped (All showed $0.00 while YTD showed $92.35), and an income row on that first date was outside `(from, to]` too. Now `all` starts the day before the first income row (a dividend or interest row, or a ledger row in the feed category) when that is earlier than the first data date, and `partial` compares with the earlier of the two; the value views keep the valuation start. Every month of the period is listed, months without income as zeros, so the chart and the table show the gaps instead of spacing March, June and August as neighbours.
- **A15. Engine warnings are sentences.** The Performance page's `readable` lines (views design §3.12 and §5) and a trade import's rebuild warnings printed `valuation_carried: 2025-11-12`. One shared `engineWarningSentence` now words every `EngineWarningCode` for the owner, with the security's ticker or name and the date (the failure reasons read as `returnReasonText` does); the structured `warnings` stay as they were.
- **A16. The day change says what it is measured from; the "why?" windows match what the row shows.** With statement-implied prices only (no provider, the default), the prior trading day often has no close and the latest earlier one is carried, so a month's move was shown as "since 10/08/26". `dayChange` now returns `carriedPrior` (the oldest prior close older than `prior` among the included lines that moved from it to a newer close, with the carried closes' sources; a stale line priced with the same row on both days moved nothing and does not count) and the Portfolio says "+$330.00 (+1.85%) since 09/09/26 (implied prices; no close on 10/08/26)", as the prices design's example asks ("the view labels the prior"); the Dashboard's investments card says the same, from `dayChangePrior`, `dayChangeAsOf` and `dayChangeCarriedPrior` on its block, where it said "+$330.00 (+1.8%) today" (second round of the audit). A row's "why?" day window runs from `prior` to the close the day change was measured at (`dayChange.asOf`), not to the page's as-of date, with a note while prices lag it; the period window of a period longer than five years is its last five years, with a note (the route's limit); and the value chart asks the series route for at most `MAX_SERIES_DAYS`, as the Portfolio's own series already did, so "All" over more than ten years no longer fails with 400.
- **A17. The expense ratio and concentration weigh what is held, and a filtered view's warnings name the account.** With a margin debit carried as negative cash (A1), the weighted expense ratio divided by the net total (0.05 % for a portfolio of one 0.03 % fund) and the concentration weight exceeded 100 %. Both now use the rows of positive value: the ratio excludes a negative cash row from both sums and concentration is a row's share of their gross value (the same as the classified total without negative cash); the table's weights still sum to 100 % of the net total. With `accountId` the weights are relative to that account, so the message says "of Vanguard Brokerage" instead of "of your portfolio".

Also fixed in this batch without a new decision: `includeClosed=true` now reaches the Portfolio's value series and the Performance route (views design D21); the Income page's account filter lists the household's investment accounts from `GET /accounts`, so it stays once one is chosen; the rebalance table prints the server's estimated-gain note once; and (second round) a Holdings row's expand toggle counts the accounts its sub-rows come from, so the Cash row's three lines from two accounts read "2 accounts", not "3 accounts".

Phase 2 and Phase 3 conformance (audit batch b9-conformance-plan-investments, findings 01, 05, 06 and 08; record in `docs/audit/phases-1-3/b9-conformance-plan-investments.md`):

- **A18. Without a price provider nothing is "behind"** (b9-08). Decision 10 keeps the app correct on implied and manual prices when `PRICE_PROVIDER=none`, but a ticker made a security `provider`-mode, so every statement-priced account was "1 trading days behind" from the day after its statement and the dashboard counted the securities as stale beside "Price feed not configured". Now the API tells the views whether a provider is configured (`priceFeed` on the valuation input of the Portfolio, Holdings, Allocation, valuation summary and series, the dashboard block and the price-history route). Without one, a `provider`-mode price older than the expected close reports its row's source (`implied`, or `manual` for a manual row) instead of `stale`, a close on the expected date is still `current`, no `price_stale` warning is raised and the Prices page counts nothing as stale; values are unchanged. With a provider the warning reads "1 trading day" in the singular.
- **A19. A return is annualized only over a year of the account's own returns** (b9-06). The lots design's D21 (GIPS: returns for periods of less than one year must not be annualized) was applied to the request's range, so an account that entered 31 days before the end of a 1-year range showed "+3.95% / +3.95% annualized". True TWR and Modified Dietz now annualize over the time the capital was invested: from the close the first computed sub-period starts at (`from` for an account valued there, the close before its first contribution or opening value for one that entered later) to `to`; under 365 days `annualized` is null and the page says "not annualized (under a year of returns)". The money-weighted cell likewise shows the period figure when the money was invested for under a year (`min(range days, investedDays) < 365`). The benchmark's own TWR is still annualized over the range (its price series covers it). The lots design's §3.9 step 4 and §3.10 are amended.
- **A20. A full-history account is valued from its trades before it has a price** (b9-05). Decision 8's chain priced lots-basis quantities only from `price` rows, so a full-history account had no value before its first statement (Portfolio "no value at 11/13/25", its whole value counted as a flow), while the Performance page valued the same lots on the same dates through the lots design's §3.6 fallback (the latest close on or before the date, else the latest `investment_txn.price`). `valueAccountOn`'s lots basis now uses the same fallback: the loader passes the full-history accounts' trade prices (`ValuationInput.tradePrices`, in stream order), and a position with no close on or before the date is priced at the account's latest trade price on or before it (`priceSource` `txn`, shown as "trade"; its status follows decision 10 and A18). A position with neither stays `missing` and the account unvalued. The day change still measures market closes only. The two engines now agree on every lots-basis date (test b9-05).
- **A21. The lots and the realized gains have pages** (b9-01). The spec's full history "unlocks lots and cost basis … unrealized and realized gains", and the lots design's §5 names a Realized gains report and a Holdings lots drawer; neither existed. **Realized gains** (`/investments/realized`, in the Investments sub-nav) reads `GET /api/investments/realized`: a calendar year (default this year) or custom dates and an account filter; one row per sale (date, account, security, shares, acquired, proceeds, basis, gain, term), unknown-basis and unmatched sales flagged and kept out of the totals, short-term, long-term and total realized, the unknown-basis proceeds, the per-account totals, transfers out listed apart (they realize nothing), and the footer "Wash sales are not applied". On Holdings, the lot count of an account's sub-row opens that security's **lots drawer** (`GET /api/investments/lots?accountId&securityId&asOf&includeClosed=true`): the open lots at the end of the page's date with acquired date and how the lot was opened, shares, basis, basis per share, value, unrealized gain and term, their totals, the closed lots collapsed, and the engine's warnings about the security.
