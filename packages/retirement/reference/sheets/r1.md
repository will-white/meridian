## 2. Rules

### 2.1 Clock, price level, ages

- The year fraction is `f = firstYearMonths / 12 = 1` in every year (the plan starts in January). A plan that starts later in the year would run its first year from `startMonth` through December with `f = months / 12`; R1 does not exercise it.
- Price level: `cpi[0] = 1` and `cpi[t+1] = cpi[t] × 1.025`, by repeated float64 multiplication. `cpi[t]` is the level at the start of plan year `t` and at the end of plan year `t − 1`. The columns `cpiStart` and `cpiEnd` print these doubles in shortest round-trip form.
- Age in a year is the age attained in that calendar year: `year − 1962`. A and B are 65 in 2027 and 85 in 2047. Both are alive in every year, so the return is joint every year and both count as filers aged 65 or older (`N_AGED = 2`).
- Months are counted as `monthIndex(year, month) = 12 × year + month − 1`. A and B were born at index 23,544.
- Early-distribution share (the 10% additional tax): `clamp((birthIndex + 714 − periodStart) / periodMonths, 0, 1)`, where 714 months is 59½ and the period of a full year starts at index `12 × year`. For A and B, 59½ was July 2021 (index 24,258), before 2027 (index 24,324), so the share is 0 for every pre-tax and Roth account in every year. No additional tax arises.

### 2.2 Markets and accounts

- Each account holds three simulated assets: stocks, bonds and bills. `us_stock` maps to stocks and `cash` to bills. R1's taxable, pre-tax and Roth accounts hold only stocks, its cash account only bills.
- Every asset returns 5.0% in every year: after the year's flows each balance is multiplied by 1.05. No fund costs.
- Rebalancing: every account is brought back to its allocation in the same trades as the year's flows. Every R1 account holds a single asset, so each trade is just the flow.
- Taxable account, average cost per asset: selling `s` from an asset with balance `V` and basis `K` realizes the long-term gain `s × (1 − K / V)` and removes `s × K / V` of basis; a purchase or a deposit adds its amount to the basis. Gains are taxed in the year of the sale.
- Investment income: dividends are 0 (the dividend yield is 0); there are no bonds, hence no coupons. Bill interest (the year's 5% on the bills held after the flows) would be taxed as ordinary interest in the following year, but the cash account is emptied in 2027 and nothing is held in bills afterwards, so taxable interest is 0 in every year.

### 2.3 The year, in order

1. Income: the pension and the year's Social Security (§2.4, §2.5).
2. Contributions and payroll tax: none (no wages).
3. Spending need (§2.6).
4. RMDs (§2.7), then the scheduled conversion (§2.8).
5. Withdrawals by the order, grossed up so that the year's cash pays the need and the tax (§2.9), with the tax computed once on the year's totals (§2.10).
6. Trades; any surplus is deposited in the taxable account at basis.
7. Returns (× 1.05).
8. The year is recorded (§2.11).

All flows of a year happen at its start, returns after; there is one tax computation per year and the tax is paid in the year.

### 2.4 Pension

12,000.00 in every year: nominal, no increase, fully taxable as ordinary income.

### 2.5 Social Security (month loop, SSA rounding)

- Full retirement age for people born in 1960 or later: 67 years 0 months (804 months). A and B reach it in January 2029 (index 23,544 + 804 = 24,348), the month of their claim.
- A claim at full retirement age has retirement factor 1: no reduction, no delayed credits.
- Primary insurance amount (PIA) at the plan start: the entered amount at full retirement age, already in 2027 dollars (`asOfYear` 2027 is the plan's first year, so no adjustment is applied before the plan): A 3,000.00, B 1,800.00.
- Cost-of-living adjustment: from 2028 on, in January of each year every PIA becomes `floorDime(PIA × (1 + COLA_Y))`, with `floorDime(z) = floor(z × 10 + 1e−9) / 10`. For a year `Y` after 2027: `P_2027 = 1`, `P_Y = P_{Y−1} × (1 + inflation of year Y − 1)`, `H` the level at the last adjustment (1 at the start); `raw = P_Y / H − 1`; `COLA_Y = max(0, floor(raw × 1000 + 0.5) / 1000)`; when `COLA_Y > 0`, `H = P_Y`. With 2.5% inflation every year, `COLA_Y = 2.5%` in every year from 2028. (The published adjustments for 2026 and 2027, 2.8% and 3.6%, are already inside the 2027 amounts and are not applied again; they are unverified figures, seams.md §9 item 1, and enter no figure of this sheet.)
- Monthly payment from the claim month on: own benefit `own = floorDime(PIA × factor)` with factor 1. Spousal excess: `max(0, 0.5 × partner's PIA − own PIA × max(1, factor))`, times the spousal factor; it is 0 for both in every year (A: `0.5 × 1,800 − 3,000 < 0`; B: `0.5 × 3,000 − 1,800 = −300.00` in 2027, and `0.5 × PIA_A − PIA_B` stays negative in every later year of the chain below). No survivor benefit (nobody dies before 2047). Payment = `floorDollar(own + spousal)`, `floorDollar(z) = floor(z + 1e−9)`.
- The year's benefit of a person is the sum of the monthly payments of the year: nothing in 2027 and 2028, twelve payments in 2029 and every later year. The year's total is the tax function's `SS_GROSS`; it is not scaled again.

### 2.6 Spending (`constant_dollar`)

- Core spending of year `t` = `130,000 × phase × cpi[t]`, with phase 1.0 for 2027 to 2036, 0.9 for 2037 to 2041 and 0.7 for 2042 to 2047. No survivor share applies (both alive).
- The goal: `40,000 × cpi[3]` in 2030 (today's dollars, never scaled by `f`).
- No healthcare and no long-term care.
- Need = core + goal. Under `constant_dollar` the whole need is the floor: a year fails only if the need cannot be paid. Planned spending = core (goals are not part of it).

### 2.7 Required minimum distributions

- Start age: 75 for people born in 1960 or later, so A and B take RMDs from 2037.
- Each pre-tax account's RMD = its balance at the start of the year (its 31 December balance) ÷ the Uniform Lifetime divisor for the owner's age that year, capped at the balance: 75: 24.6; 76: 23.7; 77: 22.9; 78: 22.0; 79: 21.1; 80: 20.2; 81: 19.4; 82: 18.5; 83: 17.7; 84: 16.8; 85: 16.0 (Publication 590-B, Appendix B, Table III). The Joint and Last Survivor table applies only to a spouse more than ten years younger; A and B are the same age.
- RMDs are withdrawn first, are ordinary income and are never penalized.

### 2.8 Roth conversions

80,000.00 in 2027 and 110,000.00 in 2028, nominal (never indexed, never scaled). In the conversion year, after the RMDs, the amount is taken from A's pre-tax accounts pro rata to `balance − RMD` (A owns one, `pre_tax_a`) and deposited in A's Roth account with the largest starting balance (`roth_a`). A conversion is ordinary income (`ROTH_CONV`) and is not a withdrawal.

### 2.9 Withdrawal order and the gross-up

- `available[a]` = the account's start-of-year balance − its RMD − its conversion out.
- Tiers, taken in this order: (1) cash accounts above the cash reserve (reserve 0, so all cash); (2) HSA up to the year's healthcare (no HSA); (3) taxable accounts; (4) pre-tax accounts whose early share is 0 (both); (5) the free part of each person's Roth (the whole balance: the early share is 0 and the five-year clock is met, because an existing Roth with a balance at the plan start counts as first funded in 2022); (6) the cash reserve (0); (7) pre-tax accounts with an early share above 0 (none); (8) Roth beyond the free part (none); (9) HSA beyond healthcare (none).
- Inside a tier each account's cap is its `available` share of the tier: accounts are drawn pro rata to `available`.
- `fill(x)`: the tiers are taken whole, in order, until `x` ends inside a tier; that tier gives each of its accounts `cap × (rest of x) / tier cap`.
- `net(x) = income + Σ RMD + Σ withdrawals − need − tax(x)`, where `tax(x)` is §2.10 on the year's totals with the withdrawals `fill(x)`: realized gains from the trades, RMDs plus pre-tax withdrawals, the conversion, the pension and the benefits.
- If `net(0) ≥ 0`, nothing is withdrawn and `net(0)` is the surplus, deposited in the taxable account (stocks) at basis. Otherwise the withdrawal is the exact root of `net(x) = 0` (net is continuous and increasing in `x`); if even everything available left `net < 0`, everything would be withdrawn and the rest is a shortfall (it never happens in R1).

**Not indexed** (fixed by statute): the senior deduction, 6,000 for each spouse 65 or older, reduced for each by 6% of AGI above 150,000, not below 0, for tax years 2027 and 2028 only (it ends after 2028 under `current_law`); the Social Security base amounts 32,000 and 44,000; the net investment income tax of 3.8% above AGI of 250,000; the capital-loss limit of 3,000.

**The computation** (Form 1040 order):

1. Capital gains: the net long-term gain (realized gains less any carried loss; never negative in R1) enters AGI and is preferential income.
2. `other` = pension + RMDs + pre-tax withdrawals + conversions + net gain (+ interest and dividends, 0 here).
3. Taxable Social Security (Publication 915 Worksheet 1): provisional income `PI = other + 0.5 × benefits`; `tier1 = min(0.5 × benefits, 0.5 × max(0, PI − 32,000), 0.5 × 12,000)`; taxable benefits `= min(0.85 × benefits, 0.85 × max(0, PI − 44,000) + tier1)`.
4. `AGI = other + taxable benefits`.
5. Deductions: standard `= standard deduction + 2 × additional`; senior `= 2 × max(0, 6,000 − 0.06 × max(0, AGI − 150,000))` in 2027 and 2028, 0 from 2029. Taxable income `TI = max(0, AGI − standard − senior)`.
6. Qualified Dividends and Capital Gain Tax Worksheet: preferential income `PREF = min(gain + qualified dividends, TI)`; ordinary taxable `ORD = TI − PREF`; ordinary tax = the rate schedule on `ORD`; at 0% `= max(0, min(TI, 0% maximum) − ORD)`; at 15% `= min(PREF − at0, max(0, min(TI, 15% maximum) − (ORD + at0)))`; at 20% = the rest; income tax `= min(ordinary tax + 15% × at15 + 20% × at20, schedule(TI))`; capital-gains tax = income tax − ordinary tax.
7. Net investment income tax `= 3.8% × min(interest + dividends + gains, max(0, AGI − 250,000))` (0 in every R1 year: AGI never reaches 250,000).
8. Total federal tax = income tax + net investment income tax.

The rate schedule: with thresholds `thr_0 = 0 < thr_1 < … < thr_6` and rates 10% to 37%, `base_0 = 0`, `base_i = base_{i−1} + (thr_i − thr_{i−1}) × rate_{i−1}`; the tax on `z > 0` is `base_i + (z − thr_i) × rate_i` for the largest `i` with `thr_i < z`.

The bracket rate column is the rate of the bracket that holds `ORD` (0 when deductions are unused, that is when `standard + senior > AGI`); the next gain rate column is 0% when `TI` is below the 0% maximum, 15% below the 15% maximum, else 20%. Both are in basis points.

### 2.11 What a row records

- Income: each person's Social Security, the pension, and their total. Wages, rental and other income are 0; so are employee and employer contributions and payroll tax.
- Spending: planned (core), goals, floor (= need), funded, core actually funded (`funded − goals`), surplus saved. Healthcare, long-term care and shortfall are 0 in every year.
- Withdrawals by account are gross (RMDs included, conversions excluded); RMDs by account; the total; the pre-tax bucket (A + B). The Roth and HSA withdrawal columns are 0 in every year.
- Investment: realized gains (dividends and coupons are 0).
- Tax: the lines of §2.10. The standard deduction column includes the additional amounts for age (`standard deduction + 2 × additional`); the senior deduction is its own column. `MAGI IRMAA` equals AGI (no tax-exempt interest); `MAGI ACA` = AGI + benefits − taxable benefits. Income tax equals total federal tax in every year (no NIIT, no penalties). Gains at 20%, NIIT, both penalties, payroll tax and the capital-loss carryover are 0 in every year.
- Ending balances by account after returns, the taxable account's basis, the pre-tax bucket, the end total, and the end total in today's dollars (`end total ÷ cpiEnd`). The cash account's ending balance is 0 in every year.
- Growth = end total − (previous end total + surplus saved − withdrawals); the first year uses the starting total 1,790,000.00. Conversions move money between accounts and do not enter it.
- Withdrawal rate (bp) = (withdrawals − surplus saved) ÷ portfolio at the start of the year; portfolio return (bp) = growth ÷ (end total − growth). Both are rounded half away from zero to whole basis points.
- The cash identity holds in every row: income + withdrawals = tax + funded + surplus saved.

### 2.12 Rounding of the printed figures

Each cell is the unrounded float64 value rounded half away from zero to the cent (`dollarsToCents`: `sign(x) × floor(|x| × 100 + 0.5 + 1e−7)`). A combined column (a bucket, a total) is the rounded unrounded sum, so it can differ by a cent from the sum of its rounded parts; the year table's `addUp` step is not applied here.
