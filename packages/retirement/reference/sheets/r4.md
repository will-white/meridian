## 1. Inputs

**Plan.** `startYear` 2027, `startMonth` 2027-01, `firstYearMonths` 12 (`f = 1`), `endYear` 2056: 30 plan years.

**Household.** One person (`p1`), born January 1962, 65 in 2027; `retirementMonth` 2027-01; longevity fixed at age 94 (alive through 2056); filing status single.

**Accounts.** One Roth account, `roth`, owner p1, 1,000,000.00, allocation `us_stock 100%`, Roth contribution basis 0.00, `expenseRatioPpm` 0.

**Income, contributions, conversions, healthcare, goals, long-term care.** None. Social Security mode `none`.

**Spending.** `constant_dollar`; base 40,000.00 a year in today's dollars; `startMonth` 2027-01.

**Run settings.** Return model `parametric` with custom parameters given as arithmetic means and volatilities (standard deviations of the simple annual rate), in basis points: stocks mean 600, volatility 1200; bonds 400 and 700; bills 300 and 200, persistence 0; inflation 250 and 100, persistence 0; every correlation 0. (Bonds and bills are unused by the account; their volatilities are non-zero because the engine's Cholesky step rejects a zero variance.) 10,000 paths; seed 1; withdrawal order `default`, cash reserve 0; `current_law`; default asset map.

## 2. Rules

- **Lognormal parameters.** For a growth factor with arithmetic mean `1 + A` and standard deviation `S`: `σ² = ln(1 + (S ÷ (1 + A))²)`, `μ = ln(1 + A) − σ² ÷ 2`. Stocks: `A = 0.06`, `S = 0.12`; inflation: `A = 0.025`, `S = 0.01`.
- **Each plan year of each path.** Draw independent standard normals `z_S`, `z_π`; stock growth `g = exp(μ_S + σ_S × z_S)`; price factor `p = exp(μ_π + σ_π × z_π)`. With persistence 0 and zero correlations, years and variables are independent.
- **No tax.** The person was 59½ in July 2021, and an existing Roth with a balance at the plan start counts as first funded five years before it (2022), so the five-year clock is met: every withdrawal is free of tax and penalty. A Roth has no RMD.
- **The year.** At the start of year `t` the need `40,000 × cpi[t]` is withdrawn (`cpi[0] = 1`); if the balance is short, the year funds what there is and the path fails (and keeps failing). Then the balance is multiplied by `g`, and `cpi[t+1] = cpi[t] × p`.
- **Outputs.** Success rate = paths that never fail ÷ paths. Ending wealth in today's dollars = the balance at the end of 2056 ÷ `cpi[30]`. Paths are ranked by ending wealth in today's dollars in cents ascending, then by the year the money ran out ascending with "never" last, then by path index; the median is the path at rank `max(0, ceil(50 × N ÷ 100) − 1)`, the p-th percentile at `max(0, ceil(p × N ÷ 100) − 1)`.
- **The independent calculator's generator** is its own: mulberry32 for uniforms with Box–Muller for normals, not the engine's counter-based generator.
