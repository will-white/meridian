## 2. Rules

### 2.1 Clock, price level, ages, filing

- The year fraction is `f = firstYearMonths / 12 = 1` in every year (the plan starts in January). A plan that starts later in the year would run its first year from `startMonth` through December with `f = months / 12`; R2 does not exercise it.
- Price level: `cpi[0] = 1` and `cpi[t+1] = cpi[t] × 1.025`, by repeated float64 multiplication. `cpi[t]` is the level at the start of plan year `t` and at the end of plan year `t − 1`. The columns `cpiStart` and `cpiEnd` print these doubles in shortest round-trip form.
- Age in a year is the age attained in that calendar year: person 1 is 69 in 2027 and 76 in 2034; person 2 is 66 in 2027 and 95 in 2056. A person is alive during the whole of the year of death and gone from the next.
- Filing status: married filing jointly from 2027 through 2034 (the year of the first death included), single from 2035. Filers aged 65 or older at the year end: 2 through 2034, 1 from 2035.
- Months are counted as `monthIndex(year, month) = 12 × year + month − 1`. Person 1 was born at index 23,496, person 2 at 23,532.
- Early-distribution shares: the 10% additional tax applies to the share `clamp((birthIndex + 714 − periodStart) / periodMonths, 0, 1)` of a pre-tax or Roth withdrawal (714 months is 59½), and the HSA's 20% to the share `clamp((birthIndex + 780 − periodStart) / periodMonths, 0, 1)` of a non-qualified HSA withdrawal (780 months is 65); a full year's period starts at index `12 × year`. Person 1 was 59½ in July 2017 and person 2 in July 2020; person 2 was 65 in January 2026. Every share is 0 in every year, so no additional tax arises.

### 2.2 Markets and accounts

- Each account holds three simulated assets: stocks, bonds and bills. `us_stock` maps to stocks, `bonds` to bonds, `cash` to bills.
- Returns in every year: stocks 6.0%, bonds 4.0%, bills 2.0%; after the year's flows each asset's balance is multiplied by its growth factor (1.06, 1.04, 1.02). No fund costs.
- Rebalancing: every account is brought to its allocation in the same trades as the year's flows. With `V'` the account's value after the flow (`V' = V − net outflow`), an asset above `weight × V'` is sold down to it and one below is bought up to it.
- Taxable account basis, average cost per asset. The account carries one basis, 340,000.00. At the start it is split as the frozen input requires: a bills slice starts with basis equal to its value (there is none here), and `max(0, basis − bills value)` is shared across stocks and bonds by their values: stocks 300,000.00 at basis 204,000.00 and bonds 200,000.00 at basis 136,000.00. Selling `s` from an asset with balance `V` and basis `K` realizes the long-term gain `s × (1 − K / V)` and removes `s × K / V` of basis; a purchase or a deposit adds its amount to basis. Gains are taxed in the year of the sale.
- Dividends: 1.5% of the taxable account's stock balance after the year's trades, all qualified, taxed in the same year, and added to the stock basis after the trades. They are part of the 6% total return: the balance is not raised by them.
- Coupons: `max(0, previous year's bill return + 1.00%)` of the taxable account's bond balance after the trades. The previous year's bill return is 2.0% in every year (for 2027, the model's first row), so the coupon rate is 3.0%. Ordinary interest, taxed in the same year, added to the bond basis; part of the 4% total return.
- Bill interest: the cash account's bills earn their 2% after the year's flows; that amount is ordinary interest (and net investment income) of the following year, and it is part of the cash account's growth. No interest is carried into 2027, and the interest of 2056 is never taxed.
- Basis is tracked only in the taxable account (cash has no gain; pre-tax, Roth and HSA balances are taxed by their own rules).

### 2.3 The year, in order

1. Income: the year's Social Security (§2.5).
2. Contributions and payroll tax: none (no wages).
3. Spending need (§2.6).
4. RMDs (§2.7), then the scheduled conversion (§2.8).
5. Withdrawals by the order, grossed up so that the year's cash pays the need and the tax (§2.9), with the tax computed once on the year's totals (§2.10), including the realized gains, dividends and coupons of the trades the withdrawal implies.
6. Trades (flows and rebalancing in one list); any surplus would be deposited in the taxable account (the largest taxable account by starting balance) at its allocation and at basis (it never happens in R2).
7. Dividends and coupons are added to the taxable basis; the cash account's bill interest is carried to next year's tax; returns.
8. The year is recorded (§2.11). At the end of 2034: the first death (§2.12).

All flows of a year happen at its start, returns after; there is one tax computation per year and the tax is paid in the year.

### 2.4 Survivor arrangements (from 2035)

- Person 1's accounts (`pre_tax_1`, `roth_1`) belong to person 2 from 2035 (spousal rollover): person 2's age sets their RMDs and their early-distribution shares, and person 1's Roth history joins person 2's. The joint accounts stay joint.
- Core spending is 70% of the couple's; healthcare is for one person.
- Person 2 receives the larger of her own benefit and the widow's benefit (§2.5).

### 2.5 Social Security (month loop, SSA rounding)

- Full retirement age (FRA) by birth year: 1958: 66 years 8 months (800 months); 1959: 66 years 10 months (802); 1960 and later: 67 years (804). Person 1 reached it in September 2024 and has been paid since then; person 2 reaches it in January 2028 (index 23,532 + 804 = 24,336), the month of her claim. Both claims are at FRA, so both retirement factors are 1: no reduction, no delayed credits.
- Primary insurance amount (PIA) at the plan start: the entered amount at FRA, already in 2027 dollars (`asOfYear` 2027 is the plan's first year, so no adjustment is applied before the plan): person 1 2,500.00 (his benefit, claimed at FRA, equals his PIA), person 2 1,500.00.
- Cost-of-living adjustment: from 2028 on, in January of each year every PIA, the deceased's included, becomes `floorDime(PIA × (1 + COLA_Y))`, with `floorDime(z) = floor(z × 10 + 1e−9) / 10`. For a year `Y` after 2027: `P_2027 = 1`, `P_Y = P_{Y−1} × (1 + inflation of year Y − 1)`, `H` the level at the last adjustment (1 at the start); `raw = P_Y / H − 1`; `COLA_Y = max(0, floor(raw × 1000 + 0.5) / 1000)`; when `COLA_Y > 0`, `H = P_Y`. With 2.5% inflation every year, `COLA_Y = 2.5%` in every year from 2028. (The published adjustments for 2026 and 2027, 2.8% and 3.6%, are already inside the 2027 amounts and are not applied again; they are unverified figures, seams.md §9 item 1, and enter no figure of this sheet.)
- Monthly payment of a living person from the claim month on:
  - own benefit `own = floorDime(PIA × factor)`, factor 1;
  - spousal excess while the partner lives and both have claimed: `max(0, 0.5 × partner's PIA − own PIA × max(1, factor))`, times the spousal factor; it is 0 for both in every year (person 2: `0.5 × 2,500 − 1,500 = −250.00` in 2027, and `0.5 × PIA_1 − PIA_2` stays negative in every later year of the chain below; person 1: negative);
  - widow's benefit, from January of the year after the partner's death and from age 60 (720 months) on: `w = PIA_deceased × max(1, d) × survivor factor`, where `d` is the deceased's factor in pay in the December of death (1 here: person 1 claimed at FRA) and the survivor factor is `1 − 0.285 × max(0, survivor FRA − age in months) / (survivor FRA − 720)`, with the survivor FRA read from the retirement table at birth year − 2 (person 2: the row for 1959, 802 months). In January 2035 person 2 is 888 months old, so the factor is 1. (When `d < 1`, `w` would be capped at `max(PIA × d, 0.825 × PIA)`; not reached.) The widow's benefit is `floorDime(w)`;
  - payment = `floorDollar(max(own + spousal, widow's))`, `floorDollar(z) = floor(z + 1e−9)`.
- The year's benefit of a person is the sum of the monthly payments of the year; it is the tax function's `SS_GROSS` and is not scaled again. Person 1 is paid twelve months in each year 2027 to 2034; person 2 nothing in 2027 and twelve months in each year 2028 to 2056, as the widow's benefit from 2035.

### 2.6 Spending (`constant_dollar`)

- Core spending of year `t` = `99,600 × s × cpi[t]`, with `s` = 1 through 2034 and 0.7 from 2035.
- Healthcare = `6,000 × cpi[t]` for each living person: 12,000 × `cpi` through 2034, 6,000 × `cpi` from 2035. It is added to the need and caps the HSA's tax-free tier (§2.9).
- The goal: `30,000 × cpi[3]` in 2030 (today's dollars, never scaled by `f`).
- Long-term care: person 2's reserve of 60,000 is spent in the last 2 years of her life, 2055 and 2056: `30,000 × cpi[t]` in each, added to the need. (Before care starts the reserve is only set aside from the spendable portfolio, which a percentage strategy would read; `constant_dollar` does not.)
- Need = core + healthcare + long-term care + goal. Under `constant_dollar` the whole need is the floor: a year fails if the need cannot be paid. Planned spending = core + healthcare + long-term care (the goal is not part of it).
- In a year with a shortfall the funded amount is allotted in the order healthcare, long-term care, goal, core; the core column shows what is left for core spending.

### 2.7 Required minimum distributions

- Start age: 73 for people born from 1951 to 1959, 75 for 1960 and later. Person 1 (1958) takes RMDs from 2031; person 2 (1961) from 2036. From 2035 both pre-tax accounts are person 2's, so 2035 has no RMD and from 2036 both accounts follow person 2's age.
- Each pre-tax account's RMD = its balance at the start of the year (its 31 December balance) ÷ the Uniform Lifetime divisor for the owner's age that year, capped at the balance. Divisors (Publication 590-B, Appendix B, Table III): 73: 26.5; 74: 25.5; 75: 24.6; 76: 23.7; 77: 22.9; 78: 22.0; 79: 21.1; 80: 20.2; 81: 19.4; 82: 18.5; 83: 17.7; 84: 16.8; 85: 16.0; 86: 15.2; 87: 14.4; 88: 13.7; 89: 12.9; 90: 12.2. The Joint and Last Survivor table applies only to a spouse more than ten years younger; person 2 is three years younger.
- In the year of death (2034) person 1's RMD is taken as usual. RMDs are withdrawn first, are ordinary income and are never penalized.

### 2.8 Roth conversions

`20,000 × cpi[1] = 20,500.00` in 2028 and `20,000 × cpi[2] = 21,012.50` in 2029 (today's dollars, never scaled by `f`). In the conversion year, after the RMDs, the amount is taken from person 1's pre-tax accounts pro rata to `balance − RMD` (he owns one, `pre_tax_1`) and deposited, at its allocation, in his Roth account with the largest starting balance (`roth_1`). A conversion is ordinary income (`ROTH_CONV`) and is not a withdrawal.

Roth state per person (it decides what a Roth withdrawal costs): the contribution basis (0 here); five yearly conversion layers (a conversion made in year `Y` joins the basis on 1 January of `Y + 5`); the first year of funding (an existing Roth with a balance at the plan start counts as first funded in 2022, five years before). A withdrawal takes basis, then the layers oldest first, then earnings; earnings are taxable unless the owner's early share is 0 and five years have passed since the first funding; the 10% tax applies to the early share of the layers and earnings taken. For both persons in every year the early share is 0 and the clock is met, so every Roth dollar leaves free of tax and penalty. At the first death person 1's basis and layers are added to person 2's, and the first year of funding is the earlier one.

### 2.9 Withdrawal order and the gross-up

- `available[a]` = the account's start-of-year balance − its RMD − its conversion out.
- Tiers, taken in this order:
  1. cash accounts above the reserve: `max(0, cash available − 20,000 × cpi[t])`;
  2. the HSA up to the year's healthcare: `min(HSA balance of living owners, healthcare)`; qualified, tax-free;
  3. taxable accounts (their gains, dividends and coupons are taxed);
  4. pre-tax accounts whose owner's early share is 0 (all of them; ordinary income);
  5. the free part of each person's Roth: `min(the person's Roth balance, free amount)`, here the whole balance;
  6. the cash reserve: the rest of the cash;
  7. pre-tax accounts with an early share above 0 (none);
  8. Roth beyond the free part (none);
  9. the HSA beyond healthcare (ordinary income, plus 20% of the early share; never reached).
- Inside a tier each account's cap is its share of the tier in proportion to its `available` balance (tiers 1 and 6 split each cash account, tiers 2 and 9 each HSA, tiers 5 and 8 each Roth account, in the same proportion): accounts are drawn pro rata.
- `fill(x)`: the tiers are taken whole, in order, until `x` ends inside a tier; that tier gives each of its accounts `cap × (rest of x) / tier cap`.
- `net(x) = Social Security + Σ RMD + Σ withdrawals − need − tax(x)`, where `tax(x)` is §2.10 on the year's totals with the withdrawals `fill(x)`: the trades they imply (sale gains; dividends and coupons on the post-trade balances), RMDs plus pre-tax withdrawals, the conversion, the carried bill interest, HSA withdrawals beyond the year's healthcare (taxable), the benefits.
- If `net(0) ≥ 0`, nothing is withdrawn and `net(0)` is saved (never in R2). Otherwise, if `net(everything) ≤ 0`, everything available is withdrawn and `shortfall = −net(everything)` (capped at the need): the year fails. Otherwise the withdrawal is the exact root of `net(x) = 0` (net is continuous and increasing in `x`).

**Not indexed** (fixed by statute): the senior deduction, 6,000 for each filer 65 or older, reduced for each by 6% of AGI above 150,000 (joint) or 75,000 (single), not below 0, for tax years 2027 and 2028 only (it ends after 2028 under `current_law`); the Social Security base amounts, 32,000 and 44,000 joint, 25,000 and 34,000 single; the net investment income tax of 3.8% above AGI of 250,000 joint or 200,000 single; the capital-loss limit of 3,000.

**The computation** (Form 1040 order; `base1`, `base2` the two Social Security base amounts of the filing status):

1. Capital gains: the net long-term gain (realized gains less any carried loss; never negative in R2) enters AGI and is preferential income.
2. `other` = RMDs + pre-tax withdrawals + conversions + coupons + carried interest + dividends + net gain + taxable HSA withdrawals.
3. Taxable Social Security (Publication 915 Worksheet 1): provisional income `PI = other + 0.5 × benefits`; `tier1 = min(0.5 × benefits, 0.5 × max(0, PI − base1), 0.5 × (base2 − base1))`; taxable benefits `= min(0.85 × benefits, 0.85 × max(0, PI − base2) + tier1)`.
4. `AGI = other + taxable benefits`.
5. Deductions: standard `= standard deduction + N × additional` (`N` aged filers); senior `= N × max(0, 6,000 − 0.06 × max(0, AGI − phase-out start))` in 2027 and 2028, 0 from 2029. Taxable income `TI = max(0, AGI − standard − senior)`.
6. Qualified Dividends and Capital Gain Tax Worksheet: preferential income `PREF = min(gain + qualified dividends, TI)`; ordinary taxable `ORD = TI − PREF`; ordinary tax = the rate schedule on `ORD`; at 0% `= max(0, min(TI, 0% maximum) − ORD)`; at 15% `= min(PREF − at0, max(0, min(TI, 15% maximum) − (ORD + at0)))`; at 20% = the rest; income tax `= min(ordinary tax + 15% × at15 + 20% × at20, schedule(TI))`; capital-gains tax = income tax − ordinary tax.
7. Net investment income tax `= 3.8% × min(interest + dividends + gains, max(0, AGI − threshold))` (0 in every R2 year).
8. Additional taxes: 10% of the early part of pre-tax and Roth withdrawals, 20% of the early part of taxable HSA withdrawals (0 in every R2 year).
9. Total federal tax = income tax + net investment income tax + additional taxes.

The rate schedule: with thresholds `thr_0 = 0 < thr_1 < … < thr_6` and rates 10% to 37%, `base_0 = 0`, `base_i = base_{i−1} + (thr_i − thr_{i−1}) × rate_{i−1}`; the tax on `z > 0` is `base_i + (z − thr_i) × rate_i` for the largest `i` with `thr_i < z`.

The bracket rate column is the rate of the bracket that holds `ORD` (0 when deductions are unused, that is when `standard + senior > AGI`); the next gain rate column is 0% when `TI` is below the 0% maximum, 15% below the 15% maximum, else 20%. Both are in basis points.

### 2.11 What a row records

- Income: each person's Social Security and their total. Wages, pensions, rental and other income are 0; so are employee and employer contributions and payroll tax.
- Spending: planned, healthcare, long-term care, goal, floor (= need), funded, core actually funded, shortfall. Surplus saved is 0 in every year.
- Withdrawals by account are gross (RMDs included, conversions excluded); RMDs by account (only the two pre-tax accounts ever have one); the total; the pre-tax and Roth buckets.
- Investment: realized gains, dividends, coupons, and the interest carried from the previous year (the cash account's bill interest).
- Tax: the lines of §2.10. `MAGI IRMAA` equals AGI (no tax-exempt interest); `MAGI ACA` = AGI + benefits − taxable benefits. Income tax equals total federal tax in every year. Gains at 20%, NIIT, both penalties, payroll tax and the capital-loss carryover are 0 in every year.
- Ending balances by account after returns; the taxable account's basis by asset (stocks, bonds; bills hold nothing) and in total, before the reset of the death year (the 2034 row shows the basis before §2.12); the pre-tax and Roth buckets; the end total; the end total in today's dollars (`end total ÷ cpiEnd`).
- Growth = end total − (previous end total + surplus saved − withdrawals); the first year uses the starting total 1,210,000.00. Conversions move money between accounts and do not enter it.
- Withdrawal rate (bp) = (withdrawals − surplus saved) ÷ portfolio at the start of the year; portfolio return (bp) = growth ÷ (end total − growth), 0 when that is 0. Both are rounded half away from zero to whole basis points.
- The cash identity holds in every row: income + withdrawals = tax + funded + surplus saved (in 2056 with `funded = need − shortfall`).

### 2.12 The first death (end of 2034)

After the year's returns, every asset of the joint taxable account moves its basis half-way to its value: `basis ← basis + 0.5 × (value − basis)` (half for a joint account, because Colorado is not a community-property state; a value below basis would move down by the same rule). Person 1's accounts and Roth state pass to person 2 (§2.4). From 2035 the return is single.

### 2.13 Rounding of the printed figures

Each cell is the unrounded float64 value rounded half away from zero to the cent (`dollarsToCents`: `sign(x) × floor(|x| × 100 + 0.5 + 1e−7)`). A combined column (a bucket, a total) is the rounded unrounded sum, so it can differ by a cent from the sum of its rounded parts; the year table's `addUp` step is not applied here.
